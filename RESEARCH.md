# BidtoList — Market Research

> Conducted: May 2026 | Target markets: Volusia County + Flagler County, FL

## The Concept

Homeowners post a listing intent (address, target date, desired price). Licensed agents submit
**blind proposals** (commission rate, CMA, marketing plan) before a deadline. After the deadline
the homeowner sees all proposals simultaneously and picks the winner. Platform charges the winning
agent a flat $295 fee.

The "blind" mechanic is the key differentiation: agents cannot see each other's bids before
submitting, creating genuine price competition rather than the anchoring dynamic on UpNest.

---

## Competitive Landscape

| Platform | Mechanic | Revenue Model | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UpNest** | Agents submit proposals; homeowner compares | 30% referral fee at closing | Acquired by Realtor.com (News Corp) 2022 |
| **Clever Real Estate** | Pre-negotiated 1.5% listing fee, agent match | 25–40% referral fee at closing | Independent, dominant SEO |
| **HomeLight** | Algorithm match only; no bidding | Referral fee at closing | ~$500M raised, well-funded |

**Key gap:** None do true blind bidding. On UpNest agents see the homeowner's address and can anchor
off each other. BidtoList's sealed-bid auction is genuinely differentiated.

**BidtoList vs UpNest post-acquisition:** UpNest absorbed into News Corp's Realtor.com has stopped
innovating. A focused, locally-branded product beats a neglected feature inside a media conglomerate.

## Market Tailwinds

### NAR Settlement (August 2024)
- Seller agent commissions now explicitly negotiated and disclosed separately
- Made commissions front-page news — homeowners now know they're negotiable
- Seller-side commission averages **$10,186** on a $368K median home
- FSBO at all-time low of **5%** — 91% of sellers use an agent

### Commission rates did NOT drop post-NAR
- 2025 surveys show combined commission actually ticked UP from 5.32% → 5.44%
- Agents are not feeling squeezed → they can afford a $295 win fee
- But homeowners ARE more aware commissions are negotiable → BidtoList pitch lands harder

## Target Market: Volusia + Flagler Counties, FL

### Market Size

| Metric | Volusia | Flagler | Combined |
|---|---|---|---|
| Licensed agents | ~7,630 | ~1,500 | **~9,100** |
| Monthly home sales | ~884 | ~180 | **~1,065/month** |
| Annual sales | ~11,985 | ~2,160 | **~14,145/year** |
| Median sale price | $343K | $358K | **~$350K** |
| Seller-side commission (2.77%) | — | — | **~$9,700/sale** |

### The Critical Ratio
**9,100 agents competing for 14,145 annual sales = 1.55 sales per agent per year.**

Most Volusia/Flagler agents close fewer than 2 homes per year. They are already paying
Zillow/Realtor.com lead fees every month. A platform where intent-based homeowner leads
come to them — and they only pay $295 on a win — is a highly compelling proposition.

### Why This Market
1. **Palm Coast = one of FL's fastest-growing cities** — steady new-listing supply from
   retirees and relocators (not just turnover)
2. **Retirement demographic** — sellers 60–75 lack confidence negotiating with agents directly;
   a marketplace where agents compete for them is psychologically appealing
3. **Price-compressed** — Daytona median $343K vs Miami/Orlando; sellers are more commission-sensitive
4. **No dominant local competitor** — UpNest and Clever have weak brand recognition in secondary
   FL markets; "find a realtor Daytona" returns generic national directories

### Seasonal Note
Volusia/Flagler peak Feb–May (snowbird season), slow Jun–Aug. **Launch in January** to ride
the spring wave.

## Revenue Math

**Revenue model: $295 flat fee on bid win, agent pays. Homeowner and agent bidding are free.**

### Path to $3k/month net

| Scenario | Closings/month | Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Conservative (1% of monthly sales) | ~10-11 | $3,000–$3,245 |
| Moderate (2% of monthly sales) | ~21 | $6,200 |
| Growth (5% of monthly sales) | ~53 | $15,600 |

At 1% capture of the combined ~1,065 monthly sales, BidtoList hits **$3k/month** — the target.
That requires ~10-11 homeowners to use the platform and have their bids accepted per month.

With 9,100 agents competing for leads and strong homeowner incentive (free service, agents come
to you), 1% capture in a focused 2-county market is achievable within 3–4 months of launch.

## Go-to-Market Sequence

**Month 1 — Agent supply:**
- Attend Flagler County Association of REALTORS meetings
- Direct outreach to agents visibly paying for leads on Zillow/Realtor.com
- Platform is free for agents until they win — zero barrier to sign up

**Month 2 — Homeowner demand:**
- Facebook/Instagram ads: "Sell your home — let agents compete for you"
- Target homeowners 55+, Volusia/Flagler zip codes: 32174, 32137, 32127, 32118, 32164
- Estimated CAC: $15–40/homeowner at this demographic

**Month 3 — First transactions:**
- First homeowners post → agents bid → collect testimonials
- Track time-to-first-bid (target: <24h with 20+ agent supply)

**Month 4–5 — Hit $3k:**
- 10-11 won bids/month at $295 = $3,000+

## Key Risks

1. **Homeowner CAC** — getting homeowners to the platform before agents is the chicken-and-egg
   problem. Solution: recruit agents first (zero-cost for them), then run paid homeowner acquisition.
2. **Commission rates didn't drop post-NAR** — agents aren't feeling squeezed enough to compete
   aggressively on price. Pitch to homeowners = "better service + transparent comparison," not
   just "lower commission."
3. **UpNest SEO dominance nationally** — addressable via hyperlocal focus (Volusia/Flagler branded
   content, local agent partnerships, community presence).
4. **90-day lag to first revenue** — home sales close 30–60 days after contract, and the $295
   fee is due on bid win (acceptance), not closing. This shortens the lag significantly.
